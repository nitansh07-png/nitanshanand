Project overview

DigiLawyer

I owned the concept. The team extended it.

I was brought in to investigate friction and weak lead generation across DigiLawyer’s service pages. Starting with the challan page, I redesigned the experience around the visitor’s immediate task: checking a vehicle’s pending challans.

The work expanded into reusable Figma components that developers implemented in code. Product owners then used those shared sections to assemble additional pages with the content and SEO teams.

    01 · Context

    Keep the content. Change what comes first.

    DigiLawyer’s product detail and listing pages contained extensive information about its legal services. During my initial audit, they often felt like articles: long explanations competed with the information visitors needed to evaluate the service and take action.

    Core challenge

    Make the service easy to start and the supporting information easy to find.

    Content and SEO needed those sections and keywords for search. So the constraint was hierarchy, not deletion.

    Where it started

    1 page

    I started with the challan page because it offered a specific task around which to organise the experience.

    02 · Problem

    The page explained the service. The form started a consultation.

    Visitors arriving to check their challans met a form asking for name, problem statement, phone number, and preferred time slot. The vehicle number was not the starting point, and violation examples, benefits and badges competed around it.

    Three problems to address

    No clear priority

    Essential service information competed with lengthy explanations and multiple messages.

    A mismatch in the first step

    Visitors needed to check a vehicle, while the form collected consultation details.

    Interaction friction

    Session recordings revealed responsiveness issues in the form, alongside repeated clicks and drop-offs.

    ?

    Design question

    How could someone begin checking their challans while still finding enough information to evaluate DigiLawyer’s service?

    Everything below is an answer to this.

    03 · Users

    Design for the task the visitor arrives with.

    I used the visitor’s task to guide each page format. These groups describe the needs addressed by the designs, rather than interview-based personas.

    Check pending challans

    What the page needed to provide: A prominent vehicle-number entry point.

    Find the right legal notice

    What the page needed to provide: Search, categories, and relevant service options.

    Evaluate money recovery help

    What the page needed to provide: Service details and situations where it could apply.

    Address an unpaid loan to a friend

    What the page needed to provide: Contextual information and a relevant enquiry form.

    The shared structure needed to accommodate these different starting points.

    04 · Pain points

    The audit found one problem. The recordings found another.

    I reviewed individual pages, watched PostHog recordings, examined interaction patterns, and compared competitor structures. Discussions with content and SEO established what the redesign needed to retain.

    Dense explanations and competing messages

    How it shaped my decisions: Prioritise the primary action and group supporting information into distinct sections.

    Visitors moving around the hero before leaving

    How it shaped my decisions: Investigate whether the offering and entry point were clear enough.

    Rage clicks and form responsiveness issues

    How it shaped my decisions: Treat interaction reliability as a separate implementation problem.

    Fewer clicks farther down the page

    How it shaped my decisions: Review where important information appeared.

    Scannable, action-focused competitor pages

    How it shaped my decisions: Explore a clearer first action and more explicit next steps.

    The recordings established friction, but did not reveal every visitor’s intent. Lack of clarity and reassurance remained hypotheses; the form responsiveness issues were directly observable.

    06 · Solution

    Four decisions, each with a cost.

    The opening ask

    4 fields before you can begin

    Clear Your Challan in Minutes!

    • Name
    • Problem statement
    • Phone number
    • Preferred time slot

    Proceed

    Describe an issue and request contact. Nothing here is the vehicle number the lookup runs on.

    Check & Pay Your Challan

    • INDVehicle number

    Check challans

    Begin with the identifier relevant to the lookup. Everything else moves to a later step.

    Schematic. An explanation of the field structure, not a screenshot of the product.

    01 Match the first input to the task

    Decision

    I replaced the consultation-style entry form with a vehicle-number field. The first input now supplied information relevant to checking challans, without asking visitors to explain their problem or select a consultation slot first.

    What the reduction covers

    The four-to-one reduction applies to the initial screen. Further information and resolution belong to subsequent steps.

    02 Make the page scannable by purpose

    Decision

    I gave the offer, reassurance, process, and reviews their own sections. Each section had a clearer job: explain the service, support confidence, describe the process, or answer a question.

    What was actually new

    The older template already contained some of this information. My decision was to change its hierarchy so visitors could locate it without reading the page as an article.

    03 Keep decisions visible. Expand the detail.

    Decision

    I kept the primary action, benefits, reassurance, and process on the main page. A concise introduction with “Read more” opened the longer explanations requested by content and SEO.

    Visible on the main page

    • Primary action
    • Benefits and reassurance
    • Process and reviews

    Available on expansion

    • Longer service descriptions
    • Detailed explanatory sections
    • Supporting tables and reference information

    Trade-off

    The key decision was what belonged in each layer. Hiding essential information would have recreated the earlier problem. The expanded view still contained substantial content; this approach changed when visitors encountered that depth. Its effect on search rankings was not separately measured.

    04 Reuse the structure. Adapt the request.

    Decision

    A vehicle number can start a challan lookup. A notice request needs context. For friendly loan recovery, the form included the amount lent and a problem statement: inputs relevant to that service.

    Why one field was the wrong rule

    The principle was a relevant first interaction. It did not require every page to use a one-field form.

    07 · Components

    Turn page decisions into reusable sections.

    After the challan redesign, I created a master collection of reusable Figma components. Developers built corresponding reusable components for implementation.

    • Service cards
    • Process steps
    • Testimonials
    • FAQs
    • Comparison sections
    • Location grids
    • Banners
    • Tables

    Consistent structure, service-specific content

    How three shared sections differ between two pages
    Shared section Challan page Friendly loan recovery page
    Process cards Three steps explaining the process. Four steps explaining what happens next.
    Testimonials Reviews on the challan page. Reviews on the notice use-case page.
    FAQs Challan-related questions. Questions about notices, delivery, and next steps.

    The component structure stayed consistent while the content, number of items, and section selection changed.

    Product owners sometimes assembled page structures from these components, then worked with content and SEO colleagues on the copy. The system supported page creation beyond my own design work.

    08 · Decisions

    What each decision gained, and what it cost.

    01 Vehicle number first

    Benefit

    A smaller, task-relevant initial ask.

    Trade-off

    Collect further information at the appropriate later step.

    02 Expandable detail

    Benefit

    Less information displayed upfront.

    Trade-off

    Keep essential information visible and the expansion control discoverable.

    03 Reusable sections

    Benefit

    Consistency across services.

    Trade-off

    Adapt forms, copy, and section selection to each task.

    04 A stable structure with visual iterations

    Benefit

    Move a clear concept toward implementation.

    Trade-off

    Further testing would be needed to compare alternative structures.

    11 · Reflection

    Give every piece of information a clear job.

    Reflection

    I learned to separate information that helps someone start, decide, or explore.

    Hierarchy and reuse are the same problem

    Working with content and SEO made that hierarchy practical. Reusable components then allowed the team to apply those decisions to other services, with forms and content adapted to each task.

    What I would measure next time

    I would define the funnel before rollout: consistent page paths, a recorded release date, and separate events for lookup starts, enquiries, and payments. I would also verify the form across devices and measure use of the expanded content. That would connect the design decisions more clearly to completed customer tasks.
