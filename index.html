Selected work

  1. Design systems Live

    One section library now assembles three live SaaS products

    My part: audited the repeat website work, defined the section taxonomy, and built the library of heroes, features, testimonials, timelines and pricing, each with its own variants, plus the specs engineering builds against.

    Launch time fell from 3 months to under 1 month.

    1. The constraintEvery new site was redesigned from scratch, so the same hero and pricing patterns were rebuilt each time at full cost.
    2. The decisionDesign the variants up front rather than per project. A section gets chosen and configured, not drawn again.
    3. The evidenceThree products now ship from the same library, and launch fell from three months to under one.
  2. AI product

    58 seconds to first useful action

    My part: designed and tested the assistant layer on a cloud-cost dashboard: the empty state, the wording, and what happens when the model is wrong.

    35+ user sessions · 4-minute average session.

    Case study ↗
    1. The constraintThe brief arrived before the product did. Nobody could yet say what the model would reliably do.
    2. The decisionDesign the empty state and the failure state first, so the interface stayed honest whatever the model returned.
    3. The evidenceAcross 35+ sessions, first useful action landed at 58 seconds.
  3. Public sector Approved

    Feedback rethought for 3.3 million daily riders

    My part: as System Design Intern, designed the station and in-transit feedback system and worked it through DMRC’s operational constraints to handoff.

    Approved by DMRC for full production rollout.

    Case study ↗
    1. The constraintThe only channel was a once-a-year survey buried on a government site, in two languages, in a city that speaks dozens.
    2. The decisionMove feedback into the journey and make it a conversation: voice or text, categorised up front so every answer arrives routable.
    3. The evidenceDMRC approved it for full production rollout.
  4. Service pages Live

    One page redesign became three page types

    My part: audited DigiLawyer’s service pages, rebuilt the challan page around its actual task, then turned that structure into master Figma components engineering built against.

    The opening ask went from four fields to one.

    Case study →
    1. The constraintThe long explanations were load-bearing for search, so the content team could not simply lose them.
    2. The decisionAsk for the vehicle number first and move the depth behind a control, rather than choosing between the visitor and the SEO strategy.
    3. The evidenceThree page types now assemble from the same sections, and product owners build pages without a designer.

How I build products

From early ambiguity to scale-stage complexity, the same five moves: find the real friction, tie it to a number, and build something the next project can reuse.

  1. Diagnose the gap

    Find where friction actually sits, in user behaviour, product logic or the tooling around the work. It is rarely where the brief says it is.

  2. Define the outcome

    Tie every design decision to something measurable: conversion, time to first action, support load, hours saved.

  3. Partner early

    Work with engineering and founders from the start, so feasibility shapes the design rather than arriving after it.

  4. Build the system

    Ship reusable patterns, not isolated screens. Tokens, master components and specs that outlive the project they came from.

  5. Validate fast

    Put it in front of real users and watch. 35+ sessions on the Costimizer assistant said more than any round of reviews.
